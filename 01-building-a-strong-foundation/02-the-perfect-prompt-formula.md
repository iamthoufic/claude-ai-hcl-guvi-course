# The Perfect Prompt Formula

- **Weak prompt = cheap result**
- **Strong prompt = better result**

## The Four-Part Prompt Framework

**Role → Context → Task → Format**

### Example 1: Fitness Coach

- **Role:** You are a fitness coach.
- **Context:** You have to help me lose 20 kg of body weight in a healthy way over the course of 6 months. My height is 6 feet 2 inches and my weight is 100 kg.
- **Task:** Help me lose the weight/fat. I want to become fit.
- **Format:** Give me the plan as a journey, flashcards, pointers, or bullet points on how to do it.

**Example of a weak prompt:**

```text
Give me a plan to lose weight.
```

### Example 2: Career Coach

- **Role:** You are a senior career coach.
- **Context:** I am a final-year CS student preparing for internships.
- **Task:** Create a beginner-friendly roadmap/career map for becoming an ML engineer.
- **Format:** Give the response as a step-by-step monthly plan.

## Why Good Prompts Save Tokens

By writing optimized, well-structured prompts, you can save plenty of tokens as well as cost.

If you run out of tokens, you either need to wait for your usage limit to reset (Claude's chat limits reset over a 5-hour session window) or spend some money to buy extra AI credits.

## Negative Instructions ("What Not to Do")

- Do not make it sound boring.
- Do not make it read like AI slop.
- Do not make it sound AI-generated.

## Positive Instructions ("What to Do")

- Add resources from the _xyz_ paper.
- Add links from _X_ on the _abc_ topic.

> **Tip:** Claude performs better with clear positive guidance. Where possible, turn a negative instruction into a positive one. For example, instead of "Do not make it sound boring," write "Use an engaging, conversational tone with concrete examples."
